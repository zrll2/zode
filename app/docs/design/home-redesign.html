Zode home, redesigned

A home that answers three questions in order: what needs me, what is working right now, what finished. Try it: approve or deny a card, tap a project chip, type in the bar at the bottom. Sample data only.

What changed and why

Answer the ask on the home screenA waiting session shows exactly what it wants to do. A slim row with the project, the title and the file it wants to change. Tick to allow, cross to deny, without opening the chat.
Running work shows its pulseEach running card prints the live step ("Editing app/settings.tsx"), a ticking timer and a moving line. You see it is alive, not just "running".
Work orders stay out of the wayConversations that start with “#” are hidden by default, so history shows your real work. One switch in the ⋯ menu gives them their own “Work orders” section below the rest, and a quiet line tells you how many are hidden.
Calm filters, no pillsThe list is short enough to read without filters. Project names are the titles.
History in plain wordsProject as the title, status as the subtitle, and one light on the left: green done, amber stopped, red failed. Nothing to decode.
The bottom bar replaces the floating buttonType the task right there. The project and mode (Plan or Edit) sit above the field. One thumb, no extra screen.
The real logo, in the headerThe app icon sits next to a rounded wordmark that echoes the rounded bars of the Z. The Mac connection is a quiet pill on the right.
